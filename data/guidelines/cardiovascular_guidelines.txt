CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE PREVENTION AND MANAGEMENT GUIDELINES
(Adapted from AHA/ACC 2019 Primary Prevention Guidelines)

1. BLOOD PRESSURE MANAGEMENT
According to the ACC/AHA 2017 Blood Pressure Guidelines, blood pressure is classified into four stages:
- Normal: Systolic < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Elevated: Systolic 120-129 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Hypertension Stage 1: Systolic 130-139 mmHg OR Diastolic 80-89 mmHg
- Hypertension Stage 2: Systolic >= 140 mmHg OR Diastolic >= 90 mmHg

For patients with Elevated blood pressure, lifestyle modifications are recommended including dietary sodium reduction, increased physical activity, and weight management. No pharmacological treatment is typically needed at this stage.

For Hypertension Stage 1, if the 10-year cardiovascular risk (ASCVD) exceeds 10%, antihypertensive medication should be initiated alongside lifestyle changes. Target blood pressure should be below 130/80 mmHg.

For Hypertension Stage 2, antihypertensive medication is recommended regardless of ASCVD risk score. Combination therapy (two drugs from different classes) may be needed.

2. CHOLESTEROL MANAGEMENT
Total cholesterol levels are classified as:
- Desirable: < 200 mg/dL
- Borderline High: 200-239 mg/dL
- High: >= 240 mg/dL

Statin therapy is recommended for patients with:
- Clinical atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD)
- LDL cholesterol >= 190 mg/dL
- Diabetes aged 40-75 with LDL >= 70 mg/dL
- 10-year ASCVD risk >= 7.5% for ages 40-75

Lifestyle modifications including a heart-healthy diet (Mediterranean or DASH pattern), regular aerobic exercise (150 minutes/week moderate or 75 minutes/week vigorous), weight management, and smoking cessation should accompany all pharmacological interventions.

3. FRAMINGHAM RISK SCORE INTERPRETATION
The Framingham Risk Score estimates 10-year cardiovascular disease risk:
- Low Risk: < 10% (lifestyle modifications sufficient)
- Intermediate Risk: 10-20% (consider statin therapy, intensive lifestyle changes)
- High Risk: > 20% (statin therapy recommended, aggressive risk factor management)

Risk factors included in the score: age, gender, total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, blood pressure treatment status, smoking status, and diabetes status.

4. LIFESTYLE RECOMMENDATIONS FOR HEART HEALTH
Physical Activity: At least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, supplemented with muscle-strengthening activities 2 or more days per week.

Diet: Emphasis on vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, whole grains, and fish. Limit sodium intake to less than 2,300 mg/day (ideally < 1,500 mg/day for hypertension). Minimize added sugars and saturated fats.

Weight Management: Maintain BMI between 18.5-24.9 kg/m². Waist circumference should be < 102 cm for men and < 88 cm for women.

Smoking Cessation: Smoking is a major modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease. Cessation reduces cardiovascular risk by 50% within one year and approaches non-smoker risk levels within 5-15 years.

Alcohol: If consumed, limit to 1 drink/day for women and 2 drinks/day for men. Excessive alcohol increases blood pressure and cardiovascular risk.

5. WARNING SIGNS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE MEDICAL ATTENTION
- Chest pain, pressure, or discomfort lasting more than a few minutes
- Pain or discomfort in the jaw, neck, back, arms, or shoulders
- Shortness of breath with or without chest discomfort
- Breaking out in a cold sweat, nausea, or lightheadedness
- Sudden numbness or weakness in face, arm, or leg
- Sudden confusion, trouble speaking, or understanding speech

Source: American Heart Association (AHA), American College of Cardiology (ACC). Guidelines for Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease, 2019.
