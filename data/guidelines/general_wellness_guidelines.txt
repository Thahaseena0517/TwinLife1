GENERAL WELLNESS AND PREVENTIVE HEALTH GUIDELINES
(Adapted from WHO, ICMR, and standard public health recommendations)

1. BODY MASS INDEX (BMI) AND WEIGHT MANAGEMENT
WHO BMI Classification:
- Underweight: BMI < 18.5 — Risk of nutritional deficiency, weakened immunity
- Normal Weight: BMI 18.5-24.9 — Ideal range, maintain through balanced lifestyle
- Overweight (Pre-obese): BMI 25.0-29.9 — Increased risk of metabolic conditions
- Obese Class I: BMI 30.0-34.9 — Moderate health risk, intervention recommended
- Obese Class II: BMI 35.0-39.9 — Severe health risk, medical supervision advised
- Obese Class III: BMI >= 40.0 — Very severe health risk, specialist intervention needed

For Asian populations (including Indians), the risk thresholds are lower:
- Overweight: BMI >= 23.0
- Obese: BMI >= 25.0
This is because Asian populations tend to have higher body fat percentages at lower BMI values.

Weight Management Strategies:
- Caloric deficit of 500-750 kcal/day for sustainable weight loss (0.5-1 kg/week)
- High protein intake (1.2-1.6 g/kg body weight) to preserve muscle mass
- Resistance training 2-3 times/week alongside aerobic exercise
- Behavioral modifications: mindful eating, stress management, adequate sleep

2. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY RECOMMENDATIONS
WHO Physical Activity Guidelines (Adults 18-64):
- Minimum: 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week
- Or: 75-150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity per week
- Muscle strengthening: Activities involving all major muscle groups on 2+ days/week
- Reduce sedentary behavior: Break up prolonged sitting every 30-60 minutes

Benefits of Regular Physical Activity:
- 30-40% reduction in cardiovascular disease risk
- 25-30% reduction in Type 2 diabetes risk
- 20-30% reduction in breast and colon cancer risk
- Improved mental health: Reduces symptoms of depression and anxiety by 20-30%
- Better bone health and reduced fall risk in older adults

3. NUTRITION AND DIETARY GUIDELINES
ICMR Dietary Guidelines for Indians:
- Cereal intake: 250-400g/day (prefer whole grains, millets)
- Fruits and vegetables: 400-500g/day (5 servings minimum)
- Protein: 60-80g/day from diverse sources (pulses, dairy, eggs, lean meat)
- Fats: 25-30g visible fat/day (prefer mustard, groundnut, or rice bran oil)
- Salt: < 5g/day (about 1 teaspoon)
- Sugar: < 25g/day (about 5 teaspoons)
- Water: 2-3 liters/day

Special dietary considerations:
- Vegetarian diets should include B12 supplementation
- Iron-rich foods for women of reproductive age
- Calcium and vitamin D for bone health (especially post-menopausal women)
- Omega-3 fatty acids for cardiovascular protection (fish, walnuts, flaxseeds)

4. SLEEP AND STRESS MANAGEMENT
Sleep Recommendations:
- Adults (18-64): 7-9 hours per night
- Older adults (65+): 7-8 hours per night
- Consistent sleep schedule improves sleep quality
- Avoid screens 1 hour before bedtime

Chronic sleep deprivation effects:
- 48% increased risk of heart disease
- 36% increased risk of colorectal cancer
- Impaired glucose metabolism (increased diabetes risk)
- Weakened immune function
- Increased cortisol and weight gain

Stress Management Techniques:
- Regular physical exercise (most effective stress reducer)
- Mindfulness meditation (10-20 minutes daily)
- Social connections and support networks
- Time management and boundary setting
- Professional counseling when needed

5. PREVENTIVE HEALTH SCREENING SCHEDULE
Annual screenings recommended:
- Blood pressure measurement (all adults annually)
- Fasting blood glucose and HbA1c (age 35+ or if risk factors present)
- Lipid profile (every 5 years from age 20, annually from age 40)
- BMI and waist circumference measurement

Periodic screenings:
- Comprehensive eye exam: Every 2 years (annually if diabetic)
- Dental check-up: Every 6-12 months
- Cancer screening: As per age-specific guidelines (mammography, colonoscopy, Pap smear)
- Thyroid function: Every 5 years from age 35

6. TOBACCO AND SUBSTANCE USE
Smoking Health Impacts:
- Doubles the risk of coronary heart disease
- 25 times higher risk of lung cancer
- Increases risk of stroke by 2-4 times
- Accelerates atherosclerosis and peripheral artery disease

Benefits of Quitting:
- 20 minutes: Heart rate and blood pressure begin to drop
- 12 hours: Carbon monoxide level in blood normalizes
- 1 year: Coronary heart disease risk drops by 50%
- 5-15 years: Stroke risk reduces to that of a non-smoker
- 10 years: Lung cancer risk drops by 50%

Alcohol Guidelines:
- If you don't drink, don't start
- If you drink: Maximum 1 standard drink/day for women, 2 for men
- One standard drink = 330 mL beer, 150 mL wine, or 30 mL spirits
- Avoid binge drinking (>= 4 drinks for women, >= 5 for men in 2 hours)

Source: World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines, Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) dietary guidelines, National Health Portal of India.
