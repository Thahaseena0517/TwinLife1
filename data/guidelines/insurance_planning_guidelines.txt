HEALTH INSURANCE ADEQUACY AND PLANNING GUIDELINES
(Adapted from IRDAI guidelines and standard insurance planning principles)

1. SUM INSURED ADEQUACY
The sum insured (SI) represents the maximum amount an insurance policy will pay for a claim:
- Minimum recommended: Rs. 5,00,000 (5 Lakhs) for individuals
- Adequate for families: Rs. 10,00,000 - 20,00,000 (10-20 Lakhs)
- Recommended for metro cities: Rs. 25,00,000+ (25 Lakhs+) due to higher hospital costs

Factors influencing adequate sum insured:
- Age and pre-existing conditions
- Family medical history
- City of residence (metro vs. non-metro)
- Lifestyle factors (smoking, sedentary lifestyle)
- Inflation in healthcare costs (medical inflation in India: 10-15% annually)

Coverage Adequacy Ratio interpretation:
- Ratio >= 1.5: Excellent coverage (sum insured exceeds estimated costs by 50%+)
- Ratio 1.0-1.5: Adequate coverage (fully covered with some margin)
- Ratio 0.5-1.0: Under-insured (significant out-of-pocket risk)
- Ratio < 0.5: Critically under-insured (immediate action needed)

2. PREMIUM AFFORDABILITY
Insurance premiums should be sustainable relative to income:
- Ideal: Premium < 3% of annual income
- Acceptable: Premium 3-5% of annual income
- Stretch: Premium 5-8% of annual income
- Unaffordable: Premium > 8% of annual income

Premium affordability ratio interpretation:
- Ratio < 0.03: Very affordable, consider enhancing coverage
- Ratio 0.03-0.05: Affordable, good balance between coverage and cost
- Ratio 0.05-0.08: Moderately strained, review if coverage can be optimized
- Ratio > 0.08: Premium burden is high, explore more cost-effective plans

Ways to reduce premiums without sacrificing coverage:
- Opt for higher voluntary deductible (co-pay)
- Choose family floater plans instead of individual policies
- Purchase at a younger age (lower entry-age premium)
- Maintain healthy lifestyle for wellness discounts
- Consider super top-up plans for high coverage at lower premiums

3. ESSENTIAL RIDERS AND ADD-ONS
Riders are supplementary benefits that enhance the base health insurance policy:

Critical Illness Rider:
- Provides lump-sum payout on diagnosis of listed critical illnesses
- Recommended for: Patients with elevated cardiovascular risk, family history of cancer
- Common covered conditions: Heart attack, stroke, cancer, kidney failure, organ transplant

Diabetes Care Rider:
- Covers diabetes-related complications and ongoing management costs
- Recommended for: Pre-diabetic (HbA1c 5.7-6.4%) and diabetic patients
- Covers: Insulin supplies, regular monitoring, diabetic retinopathy, neuropathy treatment

Hospital Cash Rider:
- Provides daily cash allowance during hospitalization
- Recommended for: Patients with hypertension or conditions requiring frequent hospitalization
- Benefit: Covers incidental expenses not covered by main policy (attendant costs, food, transport)

Personal Accident Rider:
- Covers accidental death and disability
- Universally recommended for all policyholders
- Benefit: Financial protection beyond health-related coverage

4. INSURANCE ADEQUACY SCORE INTERPRETATION
A composite score (0-100) reflecting overall insurance preparedness:
- 80-100: Excellent — Comprehensive coverage, affordable premium, all recommended riders in place
- 60-79: Good — Adequate coverage with minor gaps (e.g., one missing rider)
- 40-59: Fair — Noticeable gaps in coverage or premium strain
- 20-39: Poor — Significant under-insurance, multiple gaps
- 0-19: Critical — Severely under-insured, immediate policy review recommended

5. CLAIM PROCESS BEST PRACTICES
Cashless Claims:
- Use network hospitals for cashless settlement
- Pre-authorization required for planned hospitalizations
- Carry policy card and ID proof at all times

Reimbursement Claims:
- Submit claims within 15-30 days of discharge (per policy terms)
- Maintain all original bills, prescriptions, and discharge summaries
- Include pre-hospitalization expenses (typically 30-60 days before admission)
- Post-hospitalization follow-up costs are usually covered for 60-90 days

6. POLICY RENEWAL AND PORTABILITY
- Renew before expiry to maintain continuity benefits (waiting period credits)
- IRDAI allows portability between insurers without losing waiting period benefits
- Review sum insured annually against medical inflation (increase by 10-15% yearly)
- Declare any new pre-existing conditions at renewal to avoid claim rejection
