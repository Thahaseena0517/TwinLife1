DIABETES PREVENTION AND MANAGEMENT GUIDELINES
(Adapted from American Diabetes Association Standards of Care 2024)

1. BLOOD GLUCOSE CLASSIFICATION
Fasting blood glucose levels are classified as:
- Normal: < 100 mg/dL — No diabetes risk, continue healthy lifestyle
- Pre-diabetes (Impaired Fasting Glucose): 100-125 mg/dL — Elevated risk requiring lifestyle intervention
- Diabetes: >= 126 mg/dL — Confirmed diabetes (requires two separate tests for diagnosis)

Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) 2-hour values:
- Normal: < 140 mg/dL
- Pre-diabetes (Impaired Glucose Tolerance): 140-199 mg/dL
- Diabetes: >= 200 mg/dL

2. HbA1c (GLYCATED HEMOGLOBIN) TARGETS
HbA1c reflects average blood glucose control over 2-3 months:
- Normal: < 5.7%
- Pre-diabetes: 5.7-6.4%
- Diabetes: >= 6.5%

Treatment targets for diagnosed diabetics:
- General target: HbA1c < 7.0% (ADA recommendation)
- More stringent target: HbA1c < 6.5% for recently diagnosed, long life expectancy, no significant cardiovascular disease
- Less stringent target: HbA1c < 8.0% for elderly patients, those with limited life expectancy, extensive comorbidities, or history of severe hypoglycemia

3. DIABETES RISK FACTORS
Major risk factors for Type 2 Diabetes include:
- BMI >= 25 kg/m² (>= 23 kg/m² for Asian populations)
- Age >= 35 years (screening recommended)
- Family history of diabetes (first-degree relative)
- Physical inactivity
- History of gestational diabetes
- Hypertension (>= 140/90 mmHg or on therapy)
- HDL cholesterol < 35 mg/dL or triglycerides > 250 mg/dL
- Polycystic ovary syndrome
- History of cardiovascular disease

4. LIFESTYLE INTERVENTIONS FOR DIABETES PREVENTION
The Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) demonstrated that lifestyle intervention can reduce the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes by 58%:
- Weight loss of 5-7% of body weight
- At least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity physical activity
- Dietary pattern emphasizing whole grains, fruits, vegetables, lean proteins
- Reduction in total caloric intake, especially from saturated fats

Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT):
- Individualized carbohydrate management (no single ideal percentage)
- Emphasis on nutrient-dense carbohydrate sources
- Minimize added sugars and refined grains
- Include dietary fiber (at least 14g per 1,000 kcal)

5. PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT
First-line therapy: Metformin (unless contraindicated)
- Initiated when lifestyle modifications alone are insufficient
- Typically started at HbA1c >= 6.5-7.0% at diagnosis

Additional considerations:
- For patients with established cardiovascular disease: GLP-1 receptor agonists or SGLT2 inhibitors preferred
- Insulin therapy when HbA1c > 10% or blood glucose >= 300 mg/dL at presentation
- Regular monitoring of kidney function (eGFR) when on metformin

6. MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) for patients on insulin
- HbA1c testing: Every 3 months if not at target, every 6 months if stable
- Annual comprehensive foot examination
- Annual dilated eye exam
- Annual urine albumin screening
- Blood pressure monitoring at every visit
- Lipid profile: At least annually

7. DIABETES AND CARDIOVASCULAR RISK
Diabetes significantly increases cardiovascular disease risk:
- 2-4 times higher risk of heart disease and stroke
- Blood pressure target: < 130/80 mmHg
- Statin therapy recommended for most adults with diabetes aged 40-75
- Low-dose aspirin may be considered for those at higher cardiovascular risk

Source: American Diabetes Association. Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2024.
