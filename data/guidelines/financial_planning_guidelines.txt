PERSONAL FINANCIAL HEALTH AND PLANNING GUIDELINES
(Adapted from RBI, SEBI, and standard financial planning frameworks)

1. DEBT-TO-INCOME (DTI) RATIO MANAGEMENT
The Debt-to-Income ratio measures what percentage of gross monthly income goes toward debt obligations (EMIs, fixed expenses):
- Healthy: DTI < 0.30 (30%) — Comfortable financial position
- Moderate: DTI 0.30-0.40 (30-40%) — Manageable but limited flexibility
- Strained: DTI 0.40-0.50 (40-50%) — Financial stress zone, new debt should be avoided
- Critical: DTI > 0.50 (50%) — Serious financial distress, debt restructuring recommended

Banks and NBFCs in India typically reject loan applications with DTI above 50%. For home loans, the recommended FOIR (Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio) is below 40%.

Action Steps for High DTI:
- Prioritize paying off high-interest debt (credit cards first, then personal loans)
- Consider debt consolidation at lower interest rates
- Avoid taking on new EMI obligations until DTI drops below 0.40
- Increase income through upskilling or additional revenue streams

2. SAVINGS RATE BENCHMARKS
The savings rate represents what fraction of income is retained after all expenses:
- Excellent: >= 30% savings rate — Strong wealth-building trajectory
- Good: 20-30% savings rate — Adequate for long-term financial goals
- Adequate: 10-20% savings rate — Covers basics but limited growth
- Poor: < 10% savings rate — Insufficient for financial security

The 50/30/20 Rule (popularized by Elizabeth Warren):
- 50% of income: Needs (rent, utilities, groceries, EMIs)
- 30% of income: Wants (entertainment, dining, subscriptions)
- 20% of income: Savings and investments

For Indian households, a modified 50/30/20 approach accounts for higher savings goals needed due to limited social security infrastructure.

3. EMERGENCY FUND GUIDELINES
An emergency fund provides a financial buffer against unexpected expenses or income loss:
- Minimum: 3 months of total expenses (basic safety net)
- Recommended: 6 months of total expenses (standard guideline)
- Conservative: 9-12 months of total expenses (for freelancers, single-income households, volatile industries)

Where to park emergency funds:
- High-yield savings accounts
- Liquid mutual funds (overnight or ultra-short duration)
- Fixed deposits with premature withdrawal facility
- Avoid locking emergency funds in equity, PPF, or long-term instruments

4. FINANCIAL STABILITY SCORE INTERPRETATION
A composite score (0-100) reflecting overall financial health:
- 80-100: Excellent — Strong emergency fund, low debt, high savings rate
- 60-79: Good — Adequate reserves with room for improvement
- 40-59: Fair — Some financial vulnerabilities, needs attention
- 20-39: Poor — Significant financial stress, immediate action required
- 0-19: Critical — Severe debt burden, seek professional financial counselling

5. TREATMENT AFFORDABILITY ASSESSMENT
When evaluating whether a medical treatment is affordable:
- Affordability Score >= 1.0: Fully coverable from disposable income within the saving horizon
- Affordability Score 0.5-1.0: Partially coverable, may need to supplement with insurance or EMI
- Affordability Score < 0.5: Significant shortfall, explore insurance coverage, medical loans, or treatment alternatives

Strategies for funding expensive treatments:
1. Check insurance coverage first (sum insured vs. treatment cost)
2. Use accumulated emergency fund if non-critical to deplete
3. Explore medical loan EMI options (0% interest EMI often available for hospital tie-ups)
4. Consider phased treatment plans if medically viable
5. Government health schemes (Ayushman Bharat for eligible families)

6. INVESTMENT ALLOCATION GUIDELINES (AGE-BASED)
Asset allocation should reflect risk tolerance, which correlates with age and financial stability:

Young investors (age < 35, stability score >= 70):
- 70-80% equity (index funds, diversified equity mutual funds)
- 10-20% debt (PPF, debt mutual funds)
- 5-10% gold/alternatives

Mid-career (age 35-50, stability score >= 60):
- 50-60% equity
- 30-40% debt
- 5-10% gold/alternatives

Pre-retirement (age 50+):
- 30-40% equity
- 50-60% debt
- 5-10% gold/alternatives

7. KEY FINANCIAL RATIOS TO MONITOR
- Liquidity Ratio: Liquid assets / Monthly expenses (target: >= 6)
- Solvency Ratio: Total assets / Total liabilities (target: >= 2)
- Investment Ratio: Investment assets / Total assets (target: >= 0.50)
- Insurance Coverage Ratio: Sum insured / Annual income (target: >= 10x for life, >= 5x for health)
